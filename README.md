# Protein–Ligand Interaction Analysis of Human DHFR Antifolate Complexes
Comparative contact analysis of a classical and a lipophilic antifolate bound to human dihydrofolate reductase.

[![Pipeline](https://github.com/aposfys/protein-ligand-interaction-pymol/actions/workflows/pipeline.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/aposfys/protein-ligand-interaction-pymol/actions/workflows/pipeline.yml)
[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-green.svg)](LICENSE)

The pipeline maps every heavy-atom contact each inhibitor makes with the binding site, classifies the interactions, and quantifies which pocket residues are shared versus inhibitor-specific. Structures are [1HFR](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1HFR) (2.10 Å, hDHFR + NADPH + **MOT**) and [1KMV](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1KMV) (1.05 Å, hDHFR + NADPH + **LII**).

### Results

| Metric | MOT (1HFR) | LII (1KMV) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Contacts ≤ 5 Å | 330 | 274 |
| Residues contacted | 21 | 18 |
| Hydrogen bonds (N/O pairs ≤ 3.5 Å) | 10 | 4 |
| of which salt bridges | 7 | 2 |
| Hydrophobic contacts | 66 | 74 |
| Shortest contact | 2.25 Å (Arg70) | 2.77 Å (Glu30) |

Every row counts heavy-atom pairs, not residue pairs, so one residue can contribute several. Arg70 alone gives four of MOT's seven salt-bridge pairs, where PLIP counts two salt bridges for MOT and one for LII. The 2.25 Å Arg70 to carboxylate distance is unusually short for an N to O contact and comes from a 2.10 Å structure, so treat it as approximate.

Both inhibitors are anchored by the same pharmacophore. The 2,4-diaminopyrimidine head makes a bidentate salt bridge to **Glu30** and hydrogen bonds to the **Ile7** and **Val115** backbone carbonyls, and the two ligands share 17 pocket residues. They then diverge. MOT's L-glutamate tail carries two ionised carboxylates and reaches **Arg70**, and its benzoyl amide hydrogen-bonds **Asn64**. LII has no anion to pair with the guanidinium and packs instead against **Phe34** and **Pro61**.

[PLIP](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkab294), which types interactions from perceived chemistry rather than from distance rules, gives the same picture. **Arg70 and Asn64 appear for MOT and neither appears for LII, and Glu30 anchors both.** The pocket also agrees with the SITE records in each entry (14/14 and 10/10 residues recovered), but those records are generated by wwPDB software and match a 3.70 Å cutoff pocket, so full recall at 5.0 Å is a consistency check rather than independent evidence. The 7 and 8 further residues come from the wider 5.0 Å cutoff.

PLIP also finds two classes this pipeline cannot. The first is **π-stacking**, with Phe34 for MOT (T-shaped, benzoyl ring) and Phe31 for LII (parallel), which has no geometric rule here. The second is **water-mediated bridges**, which are invisible in principle because waters are stripped before contact detection. Both blind spots are asserted by tests. PLIP's residue counts (9 and 5) are smaller than this pipeline's (21 and 18) because it reports only typed interactions, so the two are not comparable.

The two protein chains superpose closely (PyMOL `align` RMSD 0.51 Å over all polymer atoms after outlier rejection), so the differences are ligand-driven rather than conformational. The entries differ in resolution, so sub-0.1 Å distance differences should not be over-interpreted.

### Quick start

```
pip install -e ".[dev]"   # Biopython, RDKit, PLIP, matplotlib and the test tools
make analysis             # downloads PDB files, writes results/
make figures              # PyMOL renders (requires a PyMOL install)
make test
```

The base install (`pip install -e .`) needs only Biopython. With it, run `plinter --no-chemistry`, or add `.[chemistry]` for the RDKit descriptors. PLIP and the figures are skipped when their packages are missing.

### Prior work

Human DHFR and its antifolates are among the most thoroughly characterised protein–ligand
systems in structural biology, and both entries analysed here have been in the PDB for over
two decades. The 2,4-diaminopyrimidine–Glu30 pharmacophore, the role of Arg70 in binding the
glutamate tail of classical antifolates, and the lipophilic-analogue design rationale are all
long-established. **Nothing in the results above is a new observation about DHFR.**

PLIP (Adasme et al., *Nucleic Acids Research* 2021) is the standard tool for the profiling
this pipeline does, and it is used here as an independent check rather than being
reimplemented.

This repository is a small, reproducible contact-analysis pipeline. Its discriminating
residues are cross-checked with PLIP, its blind spots (π-stacking, water-mediated bridges)
are asserted by tests rather than left implicit, and its binding-site finding is reproduced
by a different route in a different repository
([`dhfr-campaign`](https://github.com/aposfys/dhfr-campaign)). It is a teaching and
verification artefact, not a research contribution.
